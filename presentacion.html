Youth Criminal Regime (Law 27.801)

Summary

The Argentine government, through Law 27.801, officially regulated the new Youth Criminal Regime, lowering the age of criminal responsibility from 16 to 14 years old. Under this law, teenagers from 14 to 17 can face criminal charges, but they are kept in a separate system away from adults. The new rules focus mostly on education, rehabilitation, and alternative punishments like community work or electronic monitoring instead of prison, especially for minor crimes. For the most serious crimes, judges can give a maximum prison sentence of 15 years, but life imprisonment is completely banned.

Opinion

To present another point of view, I am against this new law. A law that allows a greater number of young people to be processed as adults—even though they are not yet fully developed cognitively—harms their rights much more than it helps them reintegrate into society.

Argument: System Capacity & Objectives

First, increasing the number of inmates with individuals who are still developing does not guarantee that the stated objectives will be met, even if more funds are allocated.

Conclusion

In conclusion, lowering the age of criminal responsibility while ignoring the fact that young people are still developing is another failure of the state, adding to the tragedy of a child committing a crime. The price is paid by cutting back on rights—something permanent that loses all connection to temporary emergencies.
